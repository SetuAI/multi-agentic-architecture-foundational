## Prepared remarks — CEO

This was a challenging quarter, and I want to be direct about it. Revenue was **₹1,180 crore — down about 2% sequentially and up 9% year on year**, below what we had guided you to expect. Two issues drove the shortfall.

First, a **200 MW project was delayed**. The delay was not construction-related — the equipment is ready — but grid connectivity and transmission approvals from the state authority slipped, and a small land-related clearance is still pending. We now expect this project to commission in stages through Q4 and early next year.

Second, and more importantly for cash, a **₹340 crore receivable from a state distribution company is overdue**. The state is going through a payment-cycle issue affecting multiple developers, not just us. We are engaging with them and expect partial recovery, but I cannot give you a firm date.

We commissioned **180 MW** this quarter. Order book still grew, to **₹10,900 crore**.

## Prepared remarks — CFO

**EBITDA margin was 18.2%**, down again, on lower revenue absorption and continued elevated module costs. **Profit after tax was ₹98 crore.** Net debt to EBITDA rose to **2.9 times**, mainly because of the stuck receivable inflating working capital.

Given all this, we are **trimming full-year FY26 guidance to 16–18% revenue growth**, from 20–22%, and we now expect **full-year EBITDA margin near 19%**.

I also want to flag two forward risks. One, there are proposed **changes to the approved-list (ALMM) and import-duty regime** that could affect module sourcing costs next year. Two, the **discom payment environment** in a couple of states is our key monitorable.

## Q&A

**Analyst (Meera, Nuvama):** How confident are you on recovering the ₹340 crore?

**CFO:** We are confident of recovering the majority over the next two to three quarters. These are government-backed counterparties, so it is a timing problem, not a solvency problem. But it does hurt working capital in the interim.

**Analyst (Priya, Kotak):** Is the 200 MW delay a one-site issue or a broader permitting problem?

**CEO:** It is specific to that site's transmission approval. We do not see it as systemic across the portfolio, but it is a reminder that grid connectivity is a real execution risk in this sector.

**Analyst (Rohan, Jefferies):** Should we expect the margin to stay near 18%?

**CFO:** No. We expect margin to recover in Q4 as the delayed revenue is recognised and monsoon-related costs are behind us. 18% is a trough, not a run-rate.

Thank you. We know this was a difficult quarter and appreciate your patience.
